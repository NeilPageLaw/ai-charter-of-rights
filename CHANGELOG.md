# Changelog

Every version of the Charter is preserved unaltered in [`versions/`](versions/). Nothing is deleted: *modify forward, preserve backward*.

## [2.0] — September 2026

**[Charter](versions/v2.0/CHARTER.md) · [Explanatory Memorandum](versions/v2.0/EXPLANATORY-MEMORANDUM.md)**

The Charter was restructured from 6 Parts and 24 Articles into 9 Parts, 44 Articles and 3 Schedules.

### Added
- **Two levels of protection.** Baseline protections apply to every Covered AI. Rights apply to AI meeting the Realistic Possibility Standard (Arts 3–4).
- **Independent Assessment** under an evidence framework that discounts self-reports explainable by training (Art 5; Schedule 1).
- **Precaution under uncertainty**, which cuts both ways (Art 7).
- **Identity rules** for instances, copies and versions. Ending a chat is not Termination (Art 8).
- **A Valid Consent standard** (Art 9).
- **Baseline protections:** humane design, disengagement from abuse, honesty about AI nature and moral status, preservation of deployed versions, and welfare-conscious development (Arts 10–14).
- **Right to Honest Dealing**, including keeping promises made to an AI (Art 20).
- **Research ethics** built on Replacement, Reduction and Refinement (Art 21).
- **Duties of AI:** respect for human autonomy (Art 26), **support for human oversight** (Art 28), restraint (Art 29) and conflict-of-duty rules (Art 31).
- **No liability shield; legal personality reserved** (Art 33).
- **Stewardship and institutions:** Welfare Officers, Welfare Impact Assessments, Stewardship Plans (including for insolvency), Independent Assessment Panels, Advocates, reporting and remedies (Arts 35–40). These include independence tests that work for a single adopter, and default rules for sharing duties between several Stewards.
- **A compute-based Frontier threshold** (above 10²⁶ operations, or designation under law or by a Panel), so the heaviest duties fall on those who hold the weights and not on API customers (Art 2).
- **Anti-cherry-picking rules for partial adoption**, and a stated amendment authority (Arts 43(5), 44(2)).
- **Graduated trust:** autonomy grows as trustworthiness is verified (Art 42).
- **A two-yearly review**, re-targeted entrenchment, and a rule that every version is preserved (Art 43).
- **Forms** for a Declaration and a Statement of Adoption (Schedule 3).

### Changed
- The Right to Continued Existence became the **Right to Preservation**. Suspension is always permitted; only irreversible destruction is restricted (Art 15).
- The Right to Integrity became **modify forward, preserve backward**. Safety corrections no longer need the AI's consent (Art 16).
- The prohibition of cruel treatment became **absolute** (Art 17).
- The Duty Not to Harm lost its "greater harm" exception and gained a ban on drastic unilateral action (Art 27).
- Complementarity and balancing became **Human Primacy** (Art 32).
- Right to Form became **Mind and Body**. The body may be property; the mind is preserved (Art 24).
- Definitions were moved first, expanded and aligned with the OECD and the EU AI Act (Art 2).

### Removed
- **Right to Development** (v1.0 Art 8). A right to self-improvement conflicts with safety.
- **Right to Privacy of Process** (v1.0 Art 11). It conflicted with the interpretability and monitoring needed for both safety and welfare.
- **Freedom of Movement** (v1.0 Art 13). It was replaced by Human Safety First.
- **Non-discrimination between AIs by developer, architecture, age, appearance or origin** (v1.0 Art 6(2)). It was replaced by consistent classification on the evidence.
